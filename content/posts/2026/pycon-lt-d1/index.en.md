Yesterday was the first day of PyCon LT (Lithuania): Python Day.

This is my second time attending PyCon LT, and yesterday's highlight was a "dimension-breaking" community exchange experience.

Below are the two things that impressed me most yesterday.

---

## 01. From GitHub Follow to In-Person Meeting: My "Real-Life Encounter" with Airflow Core Committer Jarek

Among the speakers yesterday, the one most familiar to many developers was probably Airflow's core Committer **Jarek**.

His talk was on the topic of how to modernize a complex Monorepo project using `uv` and `prek`.

He had previously shared this topic online; interested readers can refer to [this podcast](https://talkpython.fm/episodes/show/123/modernizing-monorepos-with-uv-and-prek).

However, for me, the "bravest" thing I did yesterday was to proactively seek him out for a chat during the coffee break.

### "Fandom" Achieved

I've been following Jarek for a long time, often seeing his active presence in PyPA and GitHub Issues.

Additionally, I previously wrote an [article about prek](2025/pre-commit-competitors), and he is one of the key figures actively promoting `prek`. (Side note: Afterward, I forwarded a tweet from Jo, `prek`'s author, on X, telling him that Jarek had mentioned `prek` again at PyCon LT. To my surprise, Jo not only liked and replied but also followed me back—double the happiness!)

**A Few Interesting Details from Our Conversation:**

*   **Finding an Entry Point:** To avoid awkward small talk, I specifically chose to approach him 10 minutes before the next session began. When he asked about me, I said I was a hobbyist open-source contributor and mentioned [cpp-linter](https://github.com/cpp-linter/cpp-linter), which I maintain.
*   **Personal Recommendation:** Jarek was clearly more interested when he heard that Apache was also using the cpp-linter project. He asked for my GitHub ID and had me type it directly on his computer. After seeing my contribution heatmap, he commented that I had made quite a lot of contributions. (Incidentally, I'm currently ranked first among public open-source contributors in Lithuania, which I guess counts as achieving something significant abroad despite the "involution".)
*   **About Airflow:** He quickly glanced at my contribution history and asked if I hadn't contributed to Airflow. I frankly admitted that I don't use it much currently but plan to delve deeper into it later.
*   **The prek Connection:** We also talked about the origin of `prek`'s name (originally `prefligit`) and its author, Jo.

Finally, I asked if we could take a photo together, and he readily agreed. Afterward, I also sent him the photo via email.

This experience of moving from online following to offline interaction was quite interesting. Over the next two days, if I encounter other big shots I've followed on GitHub for a long time, I'll continue my "fandom" attempts.

---

## 02. Mozilla's Engineering Taste: A High-Quality Talk on Toolchains

Another afternoon talk that kept me fully focused and made me pause my "Vibe Coding" was by Tadas Korris—he presented **how Mozilla chooses and uses its Python toolchain**.

### This Talk Left a Deep Impression on Me:

1.  **High Engineering Taste:**
    He wasn't just listing tools; he demonstrated Mozilla's entire toolchain from Typing, Linter, Testing, CI/CD, Monitoring, to DevOps. I've encountered most of these tools, but it was the first time I heard them presented systematically like this, which was very satisfying.

2.  **First-Hand Practical Experience:**
    As a local developer, Tadas—like many Europeans—spoke English as their second or even third language, yet it was so good and fluent, and his expression was very clear. He shared a lot of practical experience and pitfalls from his time at Mozilla, which was extremely valuable content.

An interesting detail was that Jarek, who usually codes throughout, seemed to listen very intently during this presentation. After the talk, he was the first to raise his hand to ask a question, and the two later engaged in a face-to-face discussion.

There was a sense of "resonance between masters," allowing one to feel the shared understanding and pursuit of **Engineering Excellence** among top developers.
